Skip bin permits in Dubbo: Dubbo Regional Council rules

A skip on your own driveway usually doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or nature strip is different. Here's what Dubbo Regional Council says and what to check before you book.

Last checked 24 October 2026

The short answer

On your own land, generally no. On a road, footpath or nature strip, yes: Dubbo Regional Council requires an "Approval to Place a Skip Bin in a Public Place" under section 68. Its 2025–26 fees list a $185 application fee and land rental of $1,029 per month.

On your own property

Your own driveway, yard or a firm patch of ground is the easiest place for a skip. Council's section 68 approval is for skips in a public place, so a bin wholly on your property generally doesn't need it. On rural blocks, make sure the truck has a firm, dry track in and out.

On the road, footpath or nature strip

Dubbo Regional Council requires an "Approval to Place a Skip Bin in a Public Place" under section 68 of the Local Government Act 1993, applied for through the NSW Planning Portal. Council considers the location, size and visibility of the bin. Its 2025–26 fees list an application fee of $185 and land rental of $1,029 per month for a skip in a public place.

Before you book a skip on the street, check with Dubbo Regional Council on 02 6801 4000 or council@dubbo.nsw.gov.au, or visit the council website. Fees change each financial year, so confirm the current amount. Mention it on the quote form too.

What to ask council before you book

  1. Does my spot need approval, and which one (section 68 of the Local Government Act, and whether a Roads Act section 138 consent also applies)?
  2. What does it cost this financial year, and how long does assessment take?
  3. How long can the skip stay, and are there conditions such as lights, reflectors or a set distance from the kerb?
  4. Are there extra rules for a busy road, a bus stop or a heritage area?

Ask for the answers in writing or note the date and who you spoke to.

Placing the skip safely

Keep the skip clear of driveways, hydrants, bus stops and drains, and don't fill above the rim. Given council's monthly land-rental fee for public places, it's usually worth finding room on your own property.
